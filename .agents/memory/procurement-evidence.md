---
name: Procurement evidence
description: Trust boundaries for supplier recommendations and procurement automation.
---

Never let AI-generated supplier prices, review ratings or delivery promises appear as verified procurement evidence. Purchasing recommendations must be grounded in recorded quotes, with demo data identified explicitly.

**Why:** SME owners already make urgent purchasing decisions with incomplete information; unverified AI claims would recreate the problem this product is intended to solve.

**How to apply:** When adding live sourcing, preserve source URLs, observation times, currency and unknown fields. Separate draft communications from sent messages, and internal purchase records from supplier-confirmed orders. AI should explain available evidence and uncertainty, not hide missing data.

Online supplier discovery recommendations are contact-first leads, not purchasing decisions. An advertised price does not confirm exact-model fitment, available stock, pack quantity, shipping or delivery. A human must record what the actual seller confirmed before manager approval.

**Why:** The user wants automatic nearby sourcing for parts absent from the catalog, but a Google business listing does not prove that the seller stocks that exact part. Combining unrelated marketplace listings with a local shop's reviews would create misleading procurement evidence.

**How to apply:** Keep nearby businesses separate from online listings with unknown distance or seller identity. Attribute a listing price to a nearby business only when its own non-marketplace website matches. Preserve unknowns rather than manufacturing prices or reviews, compare only like currencies, and label city-centre straight-line distances distinctly from workshop or driving distances.
